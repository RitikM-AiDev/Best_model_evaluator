Support Ticket Classification — Business & Deployment Requirements

Document purpose:
Provide the business and deployment context required to evaluate candidate machine-learning models for automated customer-support ticket classification.

1. Business Context

Northstar Support operates a customer-service platform that receives approximately 180,000 support tickets per month.

The company is evaluating an automated classifier that assigns each incoming ticket to one of four categories:

- Billing — invoices, charges, refunds, and payment questions.
- Technical Issue — product errors, outages, bugs, and troubleshooting.
- Account Access — login, authentication, locked accounts, and access recovery.
- Cancellation — subscription cancellation and related requests.

2. Business Priorities

Incorrect routing of Account Access tickets is considered particularly costly because these tickets can prevent customers from accessing their accounts.

The operations team therefore requires strong recall for the Account Access class.

The company also wants to reduce manual triage while keeping an automated system fast enough for real-time ticket routing.

3. Deployment Requirements

- Latency: P95 inference latency must be no more than 100 ms per ticket.
- Model size: The production service should preferably remain below 50 MB.
- Overall performance: Overall accuracy should be at least 0.90 on the held-out evaluation set.
- Critical-class performance: Recall for Account Access should be at least 0.90.
- Reliability: Evaluation should consider class-level performance, not accuracy alone.

4. Interpretation Notes

The requirements above are intended to guide model selection. A model that fails a hard deployment requirement should not be treated as deployment-ready merely because it has a strong overall metric.

The evaluation set is a held-out sample representative of normal incoming support traffic. The model-results file contains aggregate metrics; the error-analysis file contains selected ticket-level examples and should be used to understand the practical failure patterns.

5. Intended Audience

The final evaluation will be reviewed by the ML engineering team and the customer-support operations manager.
